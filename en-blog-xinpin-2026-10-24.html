New Product Launches
Keep Falling Flat?

The product is fine. The launch's “qi” never arrived

A year of R&D. Every test passed. Three rounds of packaging. Ad money spent. Three months after launch, sales are flat. Another price cut, another ad push — still nothing.

You think it is the market? A competitor's similar product is selling out. You think it is distribution? The shelves are stocked — nobody picks it up.

A product is a seed; a launch is the sowing. Wrong ground, wrong season, and even the best seed will not sprout.

The part that hurts: a failed launch burns more than ad spend. It burns a year of the team's morale. When the new product flops, even the next product struggles to get approved.

“Wrong feng shui, wasted effort.” When a launch will not catch fire, check two things first: the display space, and the launch date.

🎯 Three Ways a Launch Goes Cold

Way one: the hero product sits on a declining sector — the flagship display parked in the weakest corner of the showroom. Customers walk past without seeing it, or see it without remembering it — the best display surface, wasted.

Way two: a clashing launch date — the date was never chosen, just convenient. The first wave of buzz never ignites, and no amount of later spending relights it.

Way three: a chaotic delivery field — warehouse and shipping in disorder, deliveries keep going wrong, and reputation collapses before the product ever gets hot.

📉 A Real Case: A Food Product Dead for Three Months

A food company launched a new product that sat dead for three months. New packaging, lower price, livestream hosts — nothing moved.

I walked the floor: the showroom's hero display sat on the whole floor's weakest sector, directly facing the restroom door. And the launch date had been picked for convenience, not by date selection.

Not a product problem — the field and the date were both off. The display moved to the strong sector, the relaunch date was chosen by Tianxing date selection, and the second push finally took off.

✦My Approach

Step One: Hero Display on the Strong Sector — Let the Product Speak for Itself

The showroom's strongest spot belongs to the hero product alone. Customers see it the moment they walk in and remember it when they leave — that is what display is for. A great position carries its own traffic; no ad budget has to force it.

Step Two: Launch Date by Tianxing Date Selection — Ignite the First Wave in One Strike

A launch, a release, an opening — the date is the product's birth chart. The right date lets the first wave catch fire; a clashing date is wet firewood, no matter how good the product.

Step Three: Straighten the Delivery Field — Reputation Must Survive the Last Mile

Warehouse, shipping, after-sales — get their qi in order so deliveries hold steady and returns stay low. The fire lit up front must not be put out by the last mile.

✦Do the Math

One failed launch's ad spend would pay for ten field adjustments. Costlier still is the window: once a rival takes the market, winning it back costs several times the original budget.

The product already took a year of R&D. Do not let the final step — the field and the date — waste a year's work.
