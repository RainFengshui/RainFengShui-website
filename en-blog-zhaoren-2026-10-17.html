You Keep Hiring Wrong and Talent Keeps Leaving
A Pay Problem, or a Feng Shui Problem?

When the people positions are wrong, good talent can't sit still — and can't stay

Your new sales director quits after three months. Key engineers keep getting poached. HR hires around the clock — and people walk out just as fast.

Many founders blame bad luck, underpaid salaries, or a cutthroat industry.

Let me tell you something different: hiring and turnover look like money and luck on the surface, but the root is the "people positions" — where your HR works, where your key people sit, and what energy your door brings in. Get the people positions right and talent arrives on its own — and settles in.

🪑 The Three Traps That Drive Talent Away

Trap 1: HR sits on an afflicted spot. When the people in charge of hiring work in a missing corner, next to the restroom, or under a beam, the candidates they attract are restless, half-hearted, and short-stay. It's not your HR team's fault — the position itself keeps serious talent out.

Trap 2: Key seats have no backing. Facing away from the door, back to a hallway, beam overhead. People in these seats feel uneasy all day, and the first recruiter who calls takes them. In feng shui this is called "no mountain behind you, no peace in the heart" — an unsettled person never stays.

Trap 3: The door brings in chaotic energy. When the main entrance takes in the wrong energy, the whole office fills with scattered qi. Scattered qi makes scattered people — everyone is itchy to leave, most of them gone in two or three months, and your recruiting budget burns every year.

✦Rain's Solution

Step 1: Measure the people positions on site

Every office has different talent positions, and they must be measured on site based on orientation and layout. "The window seat is the best seat" is internet noise with no basis. I survey the site myself and find the real positions that hold people.

Step 2: Fix the seats, fix the energy intake

Move key staff to supported seats — back to a solid wall, open space in front. Move HR to an auspicious spot. Straighten the door's energy intake. No renovation, no layout changes, no disruption to daily work.
