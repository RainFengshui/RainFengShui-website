BUSINESS PAIN · RECEPTION

Reception Done Wrong,
Wealth Stops at the Door

The company's face — and the first gate of its energy

Many CEOs treat reception as decoration — a desk, a plant, looks fine.

Wrong. The reception is your company's face, and the first gate where its energy enters. Energy comes through the main door; the reception is its first stop. Messy, blocked, or rushed at that first stop, and wealth can't get in at all.

Same street, same building — why does one company keep rising while another cycles through staff and stays half-dead? Before blaming the market, look at the reception.

The face is the mouth of energy. A blocked mouth ruins the whole game.

🚪 Taboo One: Reception Facing the Elevator or Main Door

Elevator doors open and shut, energy rushes straight through — a classic rushing-through affliction. Wealth walks in and gets scattered: money comes, money goes, nothing stays.

Reception facing the office door sets inside and outside currents clashing. The welcome area never settles, and a visitor's first impression drops before you've said hello.

Energy fears straight lines. What can't be held can't bring wealth.

🪞 Taboo Two: No Backing, Polluted Bright Hall

A restroom, storage room, or stairwell behind the reception desk puts filth behind the company's face. Visitors sense it instantly — "this company doesn't pay attention."

An open, unsupported back leaves reception staff with no sense of belonging. Turnover rises, nobody stays.

Keep the face clean — and keep what's behind it cleaner.

💡 Taboo Three: Dim Light, Damaged Logo, Clutter Everywhere

Dim lighting = a dark bright hall. Honored guests don't enter a gloomy doorway; clients feel the chill before they speak.

A peeling, crooked, broken company logo — if you can't maintain your own face, why would a client trust you with their business?

Piled-up parcels, scattered clutter block the bright hall and dam the wealth. Details are feng shui. Clients don't read feng shui books, but they can read "mess."

✦Master Rain's Fix

Step One: Correct Position — Dodge the Rush, Take the Energy In

Keep the reception clear of the elevator and the main door. Where it can't move, buffer with a screen or plants. The desk should sit where it can gather the incoming energy — one site visit to the door's energy path tells us the spot.

Step Two: Clean the Bright Hall — Light, Order, Logo Intact

Bright, soft lighting. An orderly counter. A whole, upright logo. The cheapest fix with the fastest return — act today, feel it tomorrow.

Step Three: Secure the Backing — Support Behind the Desk

A solid wall or tall cabinet behind the desk; never emptiness, never a restroom. When reception staff sit steady, the company's energy sits steady.

✦Do the Math

Reception adjustments are mostly soft furnishings — the cost is modest.

But when a major client walks in, the first impression decides whether they sit down to talk. One messy reception can cost you the whole deal.

The face costs little. The lost deal costs everything.
