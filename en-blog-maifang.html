10 Feng Shui Tips for Home Buyers

Ten minutes of reading before you buy can save you hundreds of thousands

✦ By Master Rain ✦

✦Introduction

A home is the biggest investment most people ever make. Everyone compares location, schools, and price — but feng shui, the invisible factor that shapes your fortune after move-in, is often ignored until it's too late.

Drawing on years of on-site surveys, Master Rain distills the ten most critical feng shui checkpoints for buyers. Remember: checking feng shui before buying is ten times cheaper than fixing it afterward.

✦1. Avoid Road Rush

If the front door or bedroom window faces a straight road or T-junction head-on, rushing qi slams straight in — this is "road rush" (路冲). Residents tend toward restlessness, accidents, wealth loss, and health issues. Stand at the front door: if you see a straight road running directly at you, that's road rush.

✦2. Avoid Reverse-Bow Roads

When a road or river curves with its bow-back facing the house, it's called "reverse bow" (反弓煞) — associated with wealth loss, lawsuits, and family discord. The opposite, a road embracing the house like a jade belt, is auspicious.

✦3. Check the Facing Direction

The main door is the home's qi mouth, but its facing cannot be judged by direction alone — it must be determined by the external environment: are there mountains, water, roads, trees, towers, or tall buildings outside? Which way does the water come and go? Does the road embrace or stab back? These landform features (峦头) are the true basis for setting a door's facing. Judging by "south is always good" or "East-group people need East-group houses" is armchair theory that fails in real practice.

✦4. Avoid Rushing-Through Qi

When the front door aligns straight through to the balcony or back door with nothing in between, wealth qi "rushes straight through" and can't settle — "rushing-through qi" (穿堂煞). These layouts leak money badly. Test it at viewing: stand at the door — can you see straight out the balcony?

✦5. Prefer Square Layouts

A square, regular floor plan is ideal. A missing corner means missing fortune in that direction: missing northwest hurts the breadwinner's career, missing southwest hurts the matriarch's health, missing east affects the eldest son. A corner missing more than a third of the side is serious — think twice.

✦6. Avoid Beams Overhead

Exposed beams directly above the bed head, sofa, or desk press down on you long-term: poor sleep, mental pressure, career suppression. Best concealed by renovation; otherwise avoid placing key furniture under them.

✦7. Kitchen & Bathroom Placement

The kitchen (fire element) is one of the home's "wealth vaults" and belongs in an auspicious sector; bathrooms (water, unclean) must never sit at the house center (the central palace) — the taiji point. Filth pressing the center drags down the whole family's fortune and health. On any floor plan, locate the bathrooms first.

✦8. Light and Ventilation

Feng shui is about qi, and qi rides on light and air. A perpetually dark, damp home accumulates yin energy — residents feel drained and wealth stalls. Always view in daylight and feel whether natural light reaches the living room and bedrooms.

✦9. Survey the Surroundings

Within ~300 meters: any high-voltage towers, substations, landfills, hospitals, funeral homes, gas stations? These are "form sha" harming health and mood. Even a nice community deserves a look at what's right across the street.

✦10. Trust Your First Feeling

The last tip is the most subtle — and the most accurate: your first feeling walking in. Comfortable, bright, reluctant to leave? The qi likely suits you. Oppressed, chilly, eager to leave? Think thrice no matter the price. Intuition is the body's honest response to qi.

These ten are general principles. A specific home's fortune still requires reading the landform, flying-star chart, and the owner's bazi together — which is why on-site survey matters: blueprints can't show qi.

✦FAQ

问：Q: Should I really check feng shui before buying a home?

A home is one of the biggest investments of your life — you'll live there for years or decades. Feng shui determines whether the space supports you or drains you. A pre-purchase assessment is the cheapest risk management: it helps you avoid homes with serious flaws (road rush, missing corners, rushing-through-qi).

问：Q: I already bought the house and found feng shui problems. What now?

Most issues can be remedied through layout adjustments: a screen can cure rushing-through-qi, Taishan stones or plants can fill a missing corner, convex mirrors or qilin statues can block road rush. The key is an on-site diagnosis by a professional, then targeted remedies.

问：Q: Can feng shui be assessed for pre-construction (off-plan) homes?

Yes — and it should be. At the off-plan stage we read the community layout, building orientation, and floor plans. Catching problems early means you can still switch units or floors, far cheaper than renovating after move-in.

问：Q: Is American home feng shui different from Chinese?

The principles are the same; the details differ. US single-family homes need driveway rush, backyard slope, and overhanging-tree checks; condos need elevator and lobby qi-mouth analysis. Master Rain has years of on-site experience with American architecture.
