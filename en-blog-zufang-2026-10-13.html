Should Renters Care About Feng Shui?
The House Is Rented — Your Life Isn't

Qi doesn't check the deed — it only knows who lives there

"Why would I pay for feng shui on a place I don't even own?"

I hear this question eight times a month.

Behind it sit two assumptions: first, that a rental isn't worth the investment; second, that you can't adjust what the landlord owns.

Both sound reasonable. Both are wrong.

🏠 Myth 1: Qi Doesn't Check the Deed

Feng shui acts on the person living inside — not the name on the deed.

You sleep there eight hours a night. You eat, rest, and recover there. The qi of that space works on you every single hour.

Move into a rental with rotten qi and watch what follows: the lost job, the failing health, the roommate war. The landlord won't pay for any of that. You will.

The house is rented. Your life isn't.

🏚️ Myth 2: The Cheapest Rent Can Be the Most Expensive Rent

The U.S. has stigmatized-property disclosure rules — but plenty of landlords disclose as little as they can get away with.

Why did the last tenant leave? A promotion — or a divorce, a bankruptcy, an illness? Houses remember the people who lived in them.

If every tenant in a row went downhill, do you really want to be next? Will the money you saved on rent cover the job you lost or the health you broke?

🔧 Myth 3: "You Can't Adjust a Rental" — the Biggest Myth of All

Adjusting feng shui isn't tearing down walls. It comes down to three things: custom-made feng shui cures, placed in specific directions, at specific times.

No renovation. Nothing of the landlord's touched. When the lease ends, the cures move out with you — clean and simple.

Placed wrong, a cure becomes a poison. Placed right, it becomes medicine. I dare to promise a full refund if there's no result — because I know exactly what those three things can do.
