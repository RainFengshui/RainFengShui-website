Your House Has Sat on the Market for Two Months —
and Cutting the Price Didn't Help?

Before the next price cut, look at the house itself

What do home sellers fear most? Not a lack of showings — showings with no offers. Buyers walk in, take a lap, nod politely, say "let me think about it," and are never heard from again. You cut the price, and still nothing. Before you blame the market, remember this: in a bad market, good houses still get fought over; in a good market, some houses still sit for months. The difference is usually not the price. It's the house itself.

🏠 A real listing that sat for 61 days

Central Erin Mills, Mississauga — a semi-detached house at 2864 Westbury Court: 3 bedrooms, 3 bathrooms, over 1,500 square feet, listed at CAD $919,000, MLS W13747006. The listing says it plainly: 61 days on the market (updated September 4, 2026). Sixty-one days. Two months. Plenty of showings, no sale.

Let me be clear up front: I have never walked through this house, and from a listing alone I cannot say whether its feng shui is good or bad. And that is exactly my first point: the reason a house won't sell cannot be seen in photos or in a listing. You have to be there in person.

✦What do buyers mainly look at?

On one hand, buyers look at the house itself: is it good-looking, is it fully functional, is life convenient there, what are the amenities like, how are the schools? All of that is visible and tangible — buyers already have a checklist in mind before they even step in.

Second, the feeling the house gives. Once inside, is it comfortable? Does it feel like home? Would you want to live there for years? That feeling of home, that warmth, is the energy the house itself gives off — good energy, positive energy, not negative energy.

First, the entrance — the mouth of the house. A buyer's first step tells the truth. Is the door set straight? Is the entryway blocked? Can you see all the way through the moment you step in? When the entrance is choked, buyers want to walk back out. In business readings, the Children line stands for clients — the flow of people. If clients can't come in, money can't come in either. A house that won't sell: first ask whether buyers feel welcome the moment they step in.

Second, the bright hall and the light. Is the living room bright? Are the windows big enough? Is there a tall wall or a big tree pressing in from the front? A stuffy house makes buyers polite with their mouths and quick with their feet. "Let me think about it" is, half the time, a house that feels closed in and airless. The bright hall has to be open and lit: only when a buyer feels the space open up will they sit down and talk price.

Third, the walking lines. Do the kitchen, the bathrooms, and the master bedroom sit in a flow that makes sense? Does the front door line up straight through to the back door? A house with a straight-through draft lets money in the front and straight out the back — it can't hold anything, and it can't hold a buyer's price either. They may like the house and still push the price down. When the flow inside is smooth, buyers walk comfortably — and negotiate with confidence.

A choked entrance, a stuffy bright hall, a straight-through draft — all of these are that feeling, made concrete. When the feeling is right, the energy the house gives off is good and positive; when the feeling is wrong, it is the invisible negative energy inside the house blocking the sale — and negative energy feels oppressive, cold, a discomfort you can't quite put into words. Most of the time, what blocks a house from selling is not a door or a wall — it is that unseen negative energy. What is unseen decides what is seen.

✦An honest word first

Whether a house sells comes down to many factors: price, location, hard flaws in the house itself, market conditions — all of them count. An absurd price, a structural flaw, a bad school district or heavy taxes — feng shui cannot save any of that. Cutting the price, changing agents, retaking the photos: do all of that. Feng shui handles the invisible half: the negative energy inside the house that no eye can see and no hand can touch — whether buyers feel comfortable the moment they step in, and whether the energy is on your side when the price is discussed. The visible half and the invisible half both done, the house can move. A price cut alone only treats half the problem.

If your house is sitting unsold, don't rush into another price cut. Whether a house sells comes down to many factors — price, location, the market all count. But the invisible negative energy inside the house has to be cleared too. Clear it, and the visible sale follows.
