🤔 The doubt: team infighting — a management problem or a feng shui problem?

Management manages people — but it cannot manage energy.

When an office's energy field turns chaotic, people get restless and irritable — small things spark big fights, and backstabbers multiply.

When the energy flows right, people settle down — their energy goes into the work, not the war.

🪑 Trap one: seats with no backing — the "backstabber's seat"

An employee's desk faces away from the door, or away from the window — nothing behind them. In feng shui this is called "no backing," and it breeds backstabbers.

With people walking behind them all day, their nerves never settle, their guard never drops. People on guard don't cooperate — they fight.

📐 Trap two: missing corners — lose the corner, lose the people

A floor plan with a missing corner wounds whichever group that direction belongs to. A missing northwest wounds the owner and the core team; a missing east wounds middle management and the young backbone.

Lose the corner, and the people who belong to it never sit still for long.

💨 Trap three: energy rushing at desks — the match that lights the fights

The main door blasts straight at workstations. A hallway cuts across seats. Energy moving like an arrow — sit in its path, and tempers run shorter by the day, until nobody can stand anybody.

Half the fights in an office start with this chaotic rush.

✦Master Rain's method: fix the energy field first, then talk management

First: find the root with an on-site survey

Office size, headcount, layout — walk it, room by room. The root cause is hiding in the layout, not on anyone's face.

Second: reset seats and movement lines

Give key positions a backing and a clear view, and let the energy flow. Once the energy settles, people settle.

Third: custom objects to anchor the space

Custom-made feng shui objects, placed at specific times in specific directions — the right object, the right time, the right direction; all three required. No construction, no disruption to work.
