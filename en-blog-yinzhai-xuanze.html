How to Choose Cemetery Feng Shui

One line of yin-dwelling, three generations of fortune

✦ By Master Rain ✦

✦Introduction

A feng shui saying: "Yang dwellings govern one generation; yin dwellings govern three." Ancestral grave feng shui shapes the whole clan's trajectory — descendants, health, career, wealth all rest under its shade or drag.

Choosing a grave site isn't choosing a"pretty"place — it's choosing a place"with qi."This article explains the core standards.

✦1. Read the Dragon Vein: Qi Is Fundamental

The dragon vein is the track along which mountain-river qi flows. A good site first needs qi in the incoming dragon: ranges rising and falling rhythmically, layered guardians, like a dragon crossing the land — never a lone peak standing helpless. On plains, read water dragons: embracing, affectionate currents are good dragons.

Master Rain's fieldwork: climb, find the dragon, tread the dragon, pinpoint the lair, arrange sand and receive water, set orientation — not one step skippable.

✦2. Read the Bright Hall: The Pattern of Prospects

The open ground before the grave is the bright hall (明堂). Broad and level means descendants with vast prospects and open minds; cramped or blocked means constrained development. Water embracing the bright hall (pond, river) is luck upon luck — "mountains govern descendants, water governs wealth."

✦3. Read the Water Mouth: Wealth's Gate

Where water exits is the water mouth. It should be locked layer upon layer (hills ring, waters wind) so wealth qi stays; a mouth pouring straight out means wealth comes and goes to nothing. Walk downstream a stretch and read the mouth's form — required coursework when choosing a site.

✦4. Read the Orientation: A Hair's Breadth in Gold-Division

The trade saying: "A hair's breadth in gold-division, a world apart in fortune; I make kings and marquises, you make bandit chiefs." Orientation must coordinate with dragon, hall, and mouth, measured precisely with a luopan. Yet 90% of practitioners never mastered gold-division technique — good land becomes ruined land, disaster upon the family. Finding the right person is step one of yin-dwelling.

✦5. Avoid Form Sha

Around graves avoid: high-voltage towers, straight-road rushes, jagged rocks, collapsed or flooded mounds, termite erosion. After choosing well, maintain regularly: Qingming tomb-sweeping isn't only ancestor worship — it's the time to check the grave's condition.

Diagnosing problems is only step one; remedy is the key. Ancestral grave diagnosis plus tuning — targeted remedies restoring the grave's vitality, blessing descendants.

✦FAQ

问：Q: Cemetery plot vs. private mountain land — which has better feng shui?

Each has pros and cons. Public cemeteries are regulated and legal, but land is mass-planned — true dragon lairs are rare. Private mountain land, if a genuine dragon-vein site is found, far outperforms cemeteries — but must be legal and accessible for descendants' visits. Judge the specific site's quality, not the format.

问：Q: How is the grave's orientation chosen?

Orientation must coordinate with the incoming dragon vein, bright hall, and water mouth — 'arranging sand, receiving water, setting orientation.' The trade saying goes: 'A hair's breadth in gold-division, a world apart in fortune.' One degree off changes everything; it must be measured on-site with a luopan by someone who masters gold-division technique.

问：Q: What are signs of ancestral-grave problems?

Traditional experience: multiple descendants with the same illness, inexplicable career blocks, steady family decline, difficulty conceiving — often linked to grave feng shui. Diagnosis needs on-site inspection: collapsed mounds, water ingress, termites, surrounding structures rushing the grave.

问：Q: What matters when relocating a grave?

Relocation is major: first have a feng shui master find the new auspicious site, then select a date (avoiding Three Sha and year-breakers). Bone-collection hour, re-interment method, and burial liturgy all matter. Never break ground carelessly and disturb the ancestors.
