Three Taboos of the CEO's Office

Position decides decisions; decisions decide fate

The CEO's office is the "brain" of the company.

Every decision in the company leaves this room.

Get the decisions right, and the company takes off. Get them wrong, and tens of millions go up in smoke.

Position decides the quality of your decisions. That is not mysticism. That is common sense.

🤔 The doubt: can desk orientation really affect promotions and pay?

Most people shrug: orientation — isn't that just psychology?

Let me ask you back: can a CEO who is constantly walked past from behind, constantly interrupted mid-call, make good decisions?

Energy is simply the environment's sustained effect on a person. Sit in the wrong spot, and you are making your most important decisions inside a bad environment, every single day.

🏢 Two CEOs, two outcomes

Same ability, same industry. One CEO sails smoothly; the other drowns in lawsuits. What is the difference?

The difference is the room where the decisions are made every day. One has solid backing and a well-placed door; the other sits with his back to the entrance, staring down an elevator bank.

That is not luck. That is position. Your position works on you every single day — for better or worse, never taking a day off.

⚠️ The three taboos of the CEO's office

Taboo one: no backing behind you — the seat faces a door, a corridor, or a big window behind. People coming and going, energy unsettled. With nothing at your back, no one backs your decisions — and rivals circle.

Taboo two: direct clash — facing an elevator entrance, a restroom door, or a sharp wall corner head-on. Rushing energy strikes straight at you: lawsuits and gossip never stop, and decisions keep getting interrupted and overturned.

Taboo three: sitting in the declining sector — every floor has thriving sectors and declining ones. Sit in a declining sector, and the harder you work, the more wrong it goes.

✦Orientation is not just compass luck

Many practitioners take a compass, declare an "auspicious direction," and call it done. That is wrong.

A door's orientation — a desk's orientation — must be decided by the surrounding environment as a whole: mountains, water, roads, trees, towers, tall buildings. The landforms are what actually matter.

The same goes for the CEO's office. Setting an orientation without reading the surroundings is like driving with your eyes closed.

A real survey combines the outer landforms with the inner energy field: the surroundings set the big picture, the interior sets the details. Directions without environment are just theory on paper.
