Office Feng Shui Layout Guide

Good feng shui is the cheapest talent incentive

✦ By Master Rain ✦

✦Introduction

An office's feng shui directly shapes revenue, staff retention, and decision quality. Many bosses spend fortunes on renovation yet miss the point: misplace the reception, boss's seat, or finance room, and even the grandest décor becomes a wealth-leaking layout.

This guide covers the core principles of office layout — you'll see how high the ROI of feng shui tuning really is.

✦1. The Boss's Seat: Command the Northwest

The boss's office belongs in the northwest of the whole space (Qian trigram: heaven, leadership, authority) or due south (Li trigram: brightness, reputation).

Three iron rules for desk placement: solid backing (a real wall — never back to door or window), open bright hall ahead (the farther the view, the bigger the vision), and left (Azure Dragon) higher than right (White Tiger) (tall cabinet left, keep right low).

✦2. Reception: The Company's Qi Mouth

Reception is the company's "throat" — the qi intake. It should be bright, tidy, and lively, ideally with the company logo wall behind it (a backing mountain). Never face the elevator head-on or sit dim and cluttered — a visitor's first impression of qi decides their willingness to cooperate.

✦3. Finance Room: Quiet and Hidden

Finance is the company's "wealth vault": place it quiet, discreet, away from the street front, its door never aligned with the main entrance or elevator. Wealth dislikes exposure — the lower-profile the finance room, the better wealth is kept.

✦4. Staff Area: Backing and Bright Hall

Open-plan rules: seats need backing (no door, window, or walkway behind), open space ahead (bright hall favors promotion), no overhead beams (long-term pressure drives turnover). "Moving" roles (sales) suit spots near the entrance to catch qi; "still" roles (R&D, finance) suit deeper areas to gather qi.

✦5. Meeting Room: Gather Wind, Gather Qi

The meeting room is where decisions are made: keep it square with no missing corners; oval or rectangular tables work best (harmonious, minimal clashes). No beams above the table — deciding under pressure rarely yields good decisions.

✦6. Wen Chang Position: Boost Career Luck

Every office has a Wen Chang position (favoring study, exams, creativity, decisions). The annual Wen Chang shifts yearly with flying stars, or can be fixed via the boss's bazi. Place bookshelves there, keep it lit and tidy — an invisible boost to the team's brainpower.

Office feng shui is a system: big direction (site selection, facing) sets destiny; small layout (desks, décor) tunes details. One survey before relocating beats a year of team-building budgets.

✦FAQ

问：Q: Which direction should the boss's office face?

Prefer the northwest (Qian trigram: leadership, authority) or due south (Li trigram: reputation, vision) of the whole office space. The door must open to an auspicious sector and the desk needs a solid wall behind. Direction is the foundation; the actual layout (landform) matters more.

问：Q: In an open-plan office, how should desks be arranged?

Three rules: seats need backing (a wall or tall cabinet — never back to a door, window, or walkway), open space in front (bright hall favors promotion), and no overhead beams or sharp-corner rushes. Key roles like finance and sales get priority for auspicious spots.

问：Q: Can we have a fish tank or plants in the office?

Yes — in the right places. A fish tank (water element) suits declining sectors to activate wealth, never the wealth sector itself where it would stir fortune away. Plants suit east/southeast (wood sectors), never northwest (Qian). Wrong placement is worse than none.

问：Q: I work from home. Does the study room matter?

Yes. Place the desk at the annual Wen Chang (academic/luck) position — it shifts yearly with flying stars, or can be fixed by your bazi. Backing behind, openness ahead. A bedroom doubling as office is taboo: rest qi and work qi mix, and both suffer.
